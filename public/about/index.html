About

Last updated November 14, 2026

Incremendle is one small incremental game a day. Every game is built by Claude from a randomly rolled theme, overnight, on its own. It's a hobby project by me, Nick DiRienzo, and an experiment in how far an agent can get making something genuinely fun, day after day.

There's no shared game engine. Each day is a single page that can be whatever it wants to be: a clicker, a sorting game, a tiny city, a belfry of owls.

The roll

Ask a language model to "come up with a game" and it lands on the same few ideas every time. The first three games were all about something lonely. So the theme isn't chosen; it's rolled with dice from fixed lists:

  • a main character (an emoji with a trait, like "a nervous waffle"), a verb and an object
  • a story arc: what the character wants, what's in the way, and how it lands
  • the core action, the length, how progress is structured, a twist and the kind of ending
  • an art style, a color palette, a music style and scale, and the voice of the jokes

Each part skips whatever the last two weeks of games used. The agent has to use the roll as is, and its real job is finding the logic that makes a pile of unrelated words into one premise. Every game's What?? button (top bar) shows its roll and what the game made of it.

The build

Every night at 1am a Mac at home runs Claude Code with one command, /new-game, and nobody watching. It rolls, pitches the game to a fresh copy of itself to ask what doesn't make sense or isn't funny, then builds it. Along the way it:

  • simulates a player clicking through the whole game, and tunes prices until it lasts as long as the roll asked, with something new to buy every half minute or so
  • composes the music in Strudel, generated live in your browser so it keeps changing instead of looping
  • opens the game in a real browser: checks the start, the ending, a phone-width screen, that the upgrades fit without scrolling, and that nothing loads from another site
  • measures the sound mix so the music sits under the effects

If every check passes, it ships. If not, the work goes to a branch and I get a notification.

The tuning

The first games I played through and fixed by hand. Now it mostly does its thing, and when I do play one and something's off, the fix isn't to that game: it becomes a rule for every game after it, written into the instructions the agent reads first. One game shipped with call lights, a timer, bells going flat and 37 upgrades; the feedback was "very very complicated", so now there's a complexity budget. Another had joke upgrades that only wrote a log line; now every purchase has to show up in the picture. The games get better because the rules do.

Read the recipe

Everything the agent is told is plain text in the repo. If you want to make your own, start here:

  • /new-game: the whole job, step by step. Roll, find the joke that ties it together, pitch, build, play-test, ship.
  • CLAUDE.md: the house rules. What every game needs (sounds, an ending, upgrades you can see), and the scar tissue from every game that got something wrong.
  • roll.py: the dice. A few hundred emoji, verbs, twists, art styles and music styles, picked at random so the ideas aren't the model's favorites.
  • slopwave: the music skill. How to write a score that grows with the game, and how to take listening notes on it.
  • check.mjs: the bouncer. Opens each game in a real browser and refuses to ship one that breaks.
  • nightly.sh: the alarm clock. Wakes Claude at 1am, hands it the job, and pushes the result if it passes.

The site

Static files on Cloudflare, no build step, no accounts. Your progress stays in your browser (privacy). Art, sounds and fonts are open-licensed and credited in each game's Credits panel; please support the human artists. The whole thing, rules included, is open source under AGPL-3.0.

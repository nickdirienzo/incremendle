And the last card turned. It was not the Chariot. It was the Fool: card nought, the one at the front of every deck, which nobody at the fair had ever seen. A traveller in a little hat with a bundle on a stick, stepping out on a fine day with a small frog at his heel, and nowhere in particular to be.

"Well," said the tin soldier, who had guessed wrong, and won nothing, and whose leave was nearly over. "So that's what it was." Gilda's drawer stayed shut. He did not mind in the least. He had wondered, and now he knew, and that was what he had come for.
